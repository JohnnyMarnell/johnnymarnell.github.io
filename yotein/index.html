Quick Context
The problem: Some protein surfaces are covered in water-loving (hydrophilic) regions that are nearly impossible to design binders for — the AI keeps getting it wrong because water molecules occupy all the binding spots.

Isaac's solution: He conditioned RFdiffusion — a generative AI that "sculpts" proteins from random noise — to specifically build β-strands (flat, zipper-like structures) that pair with the target protein's own edges, forming precise hydrogen-bond networks.

Why it's huge: His method hit a 9.2% success rate, nearly 10× better than standard AI approaches. He designed binders for KIT and PDGFRα (cancer signaling proteins) and proved atomic-level accuracy with X-ray crystallography (1.9Å agreement between digital model and physical crystal). These targets were previously considered nearly undruggable.
